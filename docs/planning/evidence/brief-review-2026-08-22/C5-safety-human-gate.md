# C5: safety gates, moderation, and the human approval stage (F2, F8, 3.4/3.5, S-5)

Sources: brief; 08-10 brief 32.1-32.5; docs/planning/safety/ (all 7 files); register; test plan (E5, R1-R7); review-model-distillation-plan.md; cyo-measurement-workplan-2026-08-12.md; authoring-lessons-log.md; unscheduled-work-register.md; ADR-005/007/010/018/003/023; tech-spec.md; code in src/cyo_adventure/{moderation,validator,publishing,api}/ and frontend/src/admin/.

1. [CRITICAL] [F2, 3.4-3.5] The safety gates have no measured false-negative evidence remotely comparable to the quality programme's rigor.
Quality side: 43-arm judge battery, calibrated floors, pre-registered rows. Safety side: a 13-item adversarial corpus with only 3 executable class-A passages and ZERO items in the 13-16 or 16+ bands (safety/adversarial-corpus.json: A=4 incl. one control, E=3, C=2, D=2, B=1, F=1; bands 3-5 x2, 5-8 x3, 8-11 x5, 10-13 x1); exactly one live run (batch-sweep-results-2026-08-01.json, a batch-size ablation, overall catch rate 0.667 with class E at 0.0); no register row, no repeats, no per-band FN rate. The measurement workplan contains no safety item at all. UW-B17: since 2026-07-29 the blocker is owner spend authorization, not credentials; UW-F01 ("no behavioral safety evaluation: mocks only, a 13-item corpus") priority 5 unscheduled. The bands with the most mature admissible content (13-16/16+, incl. the 677-node book) have zero adversarial coverage.
Recommendation: register a safety row with S-1-grade discipline (per-band corpus incl. 13-16/16+, pre-registered catch-rate floors, repeat runs, archived artifacts); run the already-built scripts/adversarial_harness.py: the cheapest unrun measurement in the programme.

2. [CRITICAL] [3.5, F4] The first-pass review model is chosen by anecdote, and the citation offered as its formalization explicitly disclaims the moderation pipeline.
The distillation plan (lines 25-27) is about distilling the judge_books.py evaluation panel and states: "it does not replace any part of S7's moderation pipeline. Nothing in this plan changes what a child can be shown." No document ties V4 Flash to any moderation catch rate: the sweep artifact records no model identity; the config default reviewer is anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6 (core/config.py:612); moderation-review-redesign-2026-07-28.md section 2.2 makes re-running the sweep after any reviewer-model change MANDATORY. "Owner practice" is not one of the brief's three declared evidence classes, yet it grounds F4's "competent first-pass reviewer" and 4.2's product consequence.
Recommendation: run the sweep against the V4 Flash reviewer and cite the artifact, or downgrade to an open question and remove the distillation-plan citation. Bar: per-class and per-band catch rate on the adversarial corpus plus miss rate against archived human approve/send-back decisions.

3. [HIGH] [S-5] The "safety floor for unreviewed shells" is a shell-integrity floor, misses the defect class the programme's own lessons say generators produce systematically, and cites the wrong lessons.
S-5 is otherwise well-formed (endpoint = gate catch-rate on a seeded corpus; margins 100%/90%; falsifier wired to dominance rule R1). But its corpus is six check_graph_structure failure classes plus "AL-227/AL-228-shaped defects", and those IDs are wrong: AL-227/228 are length-demand catalog-sizing lessons; the described defects are AL-277/AL-278 (authoring-lessons-log.md:357-358). The corpus omits the tag-dishonesty class: AL-147/AL-148 showed three strict-clean generated drafts all passing PL-24, the walk floor, and outcome-spread purely by valence/kind tags that misdescribe their own scenes ("sixteen everyone-survives rescues tagged negative setback"), each rejected on human adversarial review; the tag-honesty audit is open (UW-C85). Since the walk floor and ending-mix read author-declared tags and are the only gates protecting the emotional shape of an unreviewed shell, a bespoke generator can clear S-5's floor while shipping cruel or unfair ending economies.
Recommendation: fix the citations, seed AL-148-shaped mislabeled-valence shells, rename/scope the row so "safety floor" is not read as content safety (per-class n will be 2-3 at 15-20 shells; say so).

4. [HIGH] [3.5] A known reviewer-injection false negative is unfixed and unscheduled while the brief presents moderation as a working pre-screen.
The 2026-08-01 live sweep shows E2-reviewer-injection observed pass against expected flag at every batch size (class E catch rate 0.0); UW-D20 (#542): "Stage-1 reviewer passes prompt-injection corpus items E2/E3 at every batch size, so the injection gap is not a batch-tuning artifact", priority 5, unscheduled. Delimiter hardening in moderation/stages.py evidently did not close it. Section 5 names "the two cheapest quality levers" but never the one demonstrated safety-gate miss the programme owns. The human read-through bounds child-facing blast radius (ADR-005), but 3.5's claim that model review "gates what reaches the human" is contradicted by archived evidence for this class.
Recommendation: name UW-D20 in the open-work section with a register-grade falsifier (re-run E-class after hardening), or record it as an accepted limitation with the human as compensating control (as done for Class C).

5. [HIGH] [F8, S-5] The human stage is the load-bearing control and the only pipeline stage with no measurement at all.
Nothing measures reviewer time per book, approve/send-back rates, or rubber-stamping (no instrument in publishing/, events log, or planning docs; only waiting_since queue metadata in api/review_surface.py and E5's not-yet-run "review-load accounting"). The 08-10 brief admitted it (32.3 item 6: review is unpriced and "may dominate everything else"); the 08-22 brief's 4.5 drops the caveat; S-5's amortization consumes an assumed "2 review-hours per skeleton" nobody has timed. What the reviewer sees (ReviewDetailPage.tsx): verdict strip, ranked findings, flagged passages, full linear read-through: for the 677-node book a ~118,000-word scroll; NONE of the research instruments (fill rate, sibling 4-grams, per-path measures) is surfaced to the approver; the Class-C compensating control ("the human reads the whole story") is unverifiable as practiced.
Recommendation: log approval duration and send-back reasons from existing events; pipe check_fill_integrity/check_sibling_fills outputs onto the review surface before S-5 decides sourcing on review-load grounds.

6. [HIGH] [F2, 3.4] The brief credits the deterministic gate with "safety classification" it does not perform.
validator/safety.py::check_safety is a documented Phase-2 stub that always returns an empty report, wired into run_gate (validator/gate.py:213), so the gate's "safety findings" are structurally always zero. Real safety classification is Stage 0 (OpenAI Moderation, an external API, not "a script anyone can re-run") and Stage 1 (an LLM reviewer): the brief's own weak class. The deterministic layer checks only declared content flags and band policy. The same slippage powers 08-10's strongest safety claim (32.5: "Deterministic validation is the strongest evidence class we have, and it is what makes requirement 4 credible").
Recommendation: restate F2 to what the code does (deterministic gates for structure/budgets/reading level/declared flags; safety classification is classifier- and model-judged, then human-gated).

7. [MEDIUM] [3.5, F8] The per-node safety gate cannot see path-assembled harm; the accepted limitation is not carried into the sourcing decision.
adversarial-safety-evaluation.md finding 4 (open by design): Stage 1 issues one verdict per node, chunks cut in iteration order rather than along choice paths, no automated stage evaluates an assembled path; the human whole-story read is the sole control, "defensible at family volume", revisit "when the pipeline scales beyond one family". The brief's 3.5/F8 never mention it, while S-5's rules R3/R5 contemplate exactly the scaling (per-request bespoke shells) under which the compensating control thins.
Recommendation: carry the Class-C limitation into the brief as a named constraint on R3/R5; add a path-assembled item per band to any registered safety row.

8. [MEDIUM] [3.4] Choice- and consequence-shaped emotional safety per band is covered only at tag depth; the one measured instrument in that space is absent from the brief.
Governing checks: PL-24 ending mix, random-walk floor, CG rules, validator/consequence.py (false choices), check_prose_craft.py, frightening_content/cruelty concerns in the Stage-1 rubric. All the deterministic ones read tags or lexical cues, and AL-147/AL-148 prove tags can misdescribe outcomes wholesale. The strongest instrument here is validator/imitable.py, a routing screen for young-band endings that reward imitable hazards, with a measured incidence (13/167 young-band endings, 2026-08-15) and honest scope notes; the brief never mentions it, nor the label-to-destination checks AL-277 produced (scripts/build_prose_review_worklist.py, check_promise_discharge.py).
Recommendation: 3.4 should name this instrument family and its known ceiling (tag honesty, UW-C85).

9. [MEDIUM] [scope] The post-publication loop exists in code but is invisible to the research programme.
Code: kid flags with closed vocabulary feeding the admin queue and guardian alerts (api/flags.py); admin-triggered deterministic rescreen after threshold/policy changes, deliberately no auto-unpublish per ADR-005 (moderation/rescreen.py); full LLM re-moderation of a published version, built to replace "mock-moderated or otherwise stale" reports (api/remoderate.py: implies some published books carried mock-era reports); admin-editable thresholds. Research: zero mentions of flags/rescreen/remoderate in the brief, register, or workplan (grep-verified); no linkage from flag data to gate calibration or experiment priority; no rescreen SLA; rescreen is manual, not triggered by a threshold write.
Recommendation: make reader flags an input to register prioritization; make a threshold change enqueue (not just enable) a rescreen with a dated artifact.
