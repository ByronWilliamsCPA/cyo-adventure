# C4: cost story deep dive (4.5, F7, section 1's economics claim)

Sources: vendor-comparison/README.md and runs/, evidence/skeleton-author-vendors/ (all 5 run dirs, 142 records), deepseek-v4-pro-live-fill-plan, 08-10 brief 30-31, register (Q-1, S-1, S-5), test plan E5, UW-G19, AL-327/328/333/348, PROJECT-PLAN.md, roadmap.md, ADR-003/008/010, review-model-distillation-plan, phase-2b analysis; code: generation/usage.py, cost.py, orchestrator.py, queue.py, worker.py, metered.py, providers/*, core/pricing.py, core/config.py, moderation/pipeline.py, covers/{provider,service}.py, scripts/{compare_vendors,compare_skeleton_authors,check_fill_integrity}.py.

1. [CRITICAL] [1, 4.5] The cap section 1 leans on is never stated, and the only stated business target is arithmetically threatened by the flagship long book.
No per-book cap exists anywhere. On record: PROJECT-PLAN.md:1414 ("LLM + hosting spend per subscribing family under 20% of subscription revenue", verified by P9-05 dashboards that do not exist yet); family tier "~$5-8/month" (PROJECT-PLAN.md:1169-1170); ADR-008/010's stale "cents per story" on the retired Haiku-primary roster. The 20% target implies $1.00-1.60/family/month for ALL LLM plus hosting. Measured: the live V4 Pro run cost $0.350 (8-11), $0.538 (13-16), $1.064 (16+) per fill, delivering only 38.9-52.9% of commissioned words. A compliant 16+ fill projects to roughly $2.5-4 (book 1 spent 154,253 output tokens for 19,423 delivered words, 7.9 tok/word; 49,953 words implies ~397k output tokens, over the azure/us 384k ceiling, forcing the chunked path and multiplying the ~99k-token input); the ~118k-word tail book projects ~$6-10. One tail book is 4-6x an entire family-month allowance before repairs, review, covers, or the human. Q-1 says families request repeatedly. Nobody has done this multiplication.
Recommendation: state the per-book cap by band, derived from the 20% metric, tier price, and requests/family/month; publish it in the brief that claims it exists.

2. [CRITICAL] [4.5, F4, F7] Subagent "zero marginal provider cost" is an accounting fiction contaminating the 90% headline, the model policy, and reproducibility.
Every tool-assisted record carries input_tokens: None, output_tokens: None for all 41 shells, including metered-capable DeepSeek and Kimi legs; the four Anthropic subagent legs in the blind run are likewise all None. The regime experiment behind F3/F4 recorded zero consumption anywhere. The 90% comparison contrasts metered API dollars against unmetered subscription tokens ($4.43/$3.52/$3.38 and 89.6% reproduced exactly from smoke+e1 records at core/pricing.py rates: a genuine strength), but the "replacement slate" side has no priceable record. Shadow arithmetic: blind Sonnet 5 averaged ~106k output tokens/shell; register estimates hard-cell shells at 100-350k tokens; at the repo's openrouter Sonnet 5 rate ($2/$10) that is $1-3.5/shell, several-fold higher at Opus/Fable tiers, which have no price rows in the repo at all. F7's Haiku claim rests on 1/3 at cell D: at that acceptance each accepted hard shell costs ~3 sessions plus ~3 promotion-review cycles (2 review-hours each per the S-5 basis), making Haiku the expensive option under any accounting that prices the human. A third party without a Claude subscription cannot reproduce the slate at anything like the reported cost.
Recommendation: record per-session token usage for subagent legs; report two columns, subscription-marginal and API-shadow-priced, following the honest pattern pricing.py already uses for Ollama ("zero VENDOR cost, not zero cost").

3. [HIGH] [4.1, 4.5, F4] The recommended fill model cannot currently produce a floor-passing mid/large-band book, so every quoted $/book is the price of a hollow book and the scrap-rate term is unknown or 100%.
The brief recommends "fill with V4 Pro" while its own evidence (AL-490/UW-C307) shows 39-53% delivery at every band above 5-8. The delivery floor built in response (_DEFAULT_MIN_FILL_RATE = 0.6) lives in a script only: no call from generation/worker.py or validator/; the persisted per-book metric fill_completeness reads 1.0 on the 38.9% book because it counts filled NODES, not words. With the floor enforced, current scrap at 8-11+ is 100% and $/published book is undefined; without it, under-delivered books flow to the human and the measured figures price a defective product. Either way 4.5's numbers are not unit costs.
Recommendation: wire the floor into the production gate, fix the words= defect, re-measure $/gate-passing book per band; until then mark every per-book figure "under-delivery, pre-floor".

4. [HIGH] [4.5, F7] The AL-328 countermeasures are harness-only, "credits checks" do not exist even there, and the production queue's real exposure is the unpinned endpoint, not the exhausted balance.
Verified: preflight (compare_vendors.py:1744,1939) and --resume (compare_skeleton_authors.py:902) exist in the harnesses; a credits/balance check exists in NEITHER (grep finds only the comment narrating the 402 incident), so 4.5's "credits checks" is overstated; preflight probes reachability, not balance. Production has bounded repairs (max_repairs=3 shared), caps via resolve_output_cap, 402 classified leg-fatal, deliberate no-auto-re-enqueue of dead jobs. It has no balance preflight, no daily spend cap, no circuit breaker (deferred to P9-05, post-launch). The sharper live exposure is the live-fill plan's F2: production never pins (build_openrouter_leg provider_order defaults to (), provider.py:398; no config field; plan's open item 8.2 unanswered) while one slug's endpoints span 16,384-1,048,576 output ceilings and $0.66-1.91 input pricing. An unpinned route to a low-ceiling reseller produces billed non-empty truncations burning 1 fill plus 3 repairs per job, and prices recorded from the azure/us row are wrong for whatever endpoint served.
Recommendation: config-level endpoint pin plus a startup credits probe and a per-day spend counter over the existing cost_usd column; file the 8.2 answer as the UW row the plan requested.

5. [HIGH] [3.3, 4.5] Production cost telemetry is per-job-only, reads to nowhere, and two planned production legs would flip the fleet back to incomplete accounting.
Good: worker.py:255-264 persists usage, cost_usd, cost_complete per job; moderation review calls billed into the same job ledger. But: TokenUsage carries no stage field and the per-call list is dropped at persistence, so fill vs repair vs review spend is unrecoverable whenever stages share a model; no api/*.py reads cost_usd (zero rollup, dashboard, or regression alert; events/ has no cost events); covers/ is entirely unmetered (no usage/cost in covers/provider.py or service.py, no google-genai price row); core/pricing.py holds rows only for ("openrouter", ...) and one ("ollama", ...), so provider="anthropic" (direct, WS-C) and provider="modal" jobs persist cost_usd $0 with cost_complete False. P9-04 plans the production reviewer on Modal, which would mark every production job incomplete: the AL-333 failure shape one layer up. "Response-level where the provider reports it" is fair: all four adapters extract usage; an unreporting backend yields None, flagged, never tokenizer-guessed or silently zero.
Recommendation: stage tag on TokenUsage; persist per-call rows or per-stage subtotals; price anthropic/modal (Modal as $/GPU-second per call); meter covers; one-query rollup with an alert threshold.

6. [HIGH] [F7] "Measured levers, in order of impact" is neither a common-denominator ranking nor complete; omissions include the one lever the programme already measured.
F7 mixes incommensurable units (regime in passes, caps in dollars) with no denominator. Omitted levers with in-repo evidence: prompt caching (44% of GPT-5.6's and 30% of GLM's prompt tokens served from cache, vendor-comparison/README.md:245-249, with the warning that a price model without a cache-hit term "will over-recover on the high-volume path"; the fill prompt is mostly static, yet neither F7 nor pricing.py has a cache term); reasoning-share as a selection criterion (their cleanest discriminator: every leg above 10% share is ~2x underprojected, spread 36x per delivered book; reasoning_tokens already captured "so a leg can be selected on it", yet F7 only sizes caps around it); batch/off-peak APIs for catalog-time work (all skeleton and mutation work is offline by design, never discussed); chunk-size economics (chunked path never costed or validated live, plan F1); salvaging validated partial fills instead of whole-book scrap; premium-tier tokens only on decision nodes vs corridor nodes (natural fit to F5's stratified plan); Modal at scale (ADR-010's "10-50x" GPU-vs-token claim is a stated assumption with no measurement). Review-model distillation is correctly not claimed as a cost lever.
Recommendation: re-rank F7 in $/published-book terms once finding 1's model exists; add caching and reasoning-share now (both already measured).

7. [MEDIUM] [4.2 consequence, 3.5] The recommended per-stage assignment concentrates fill and review in one vendor, contradicting ADR-010's own independence rationale; the commercial residue is unassessed.
ADR-010:49-51: the reviewer "should be a different model family than the generator (independent blind spots)". Same-family review weakens the property the review stage exists for, and concentrates deprecation, ToS, and outage risk in one lab across two stages. Data handling is genuinely covered (ADR-003 ZDR/no-training; azure/us pin on residency; first-party DeepSeek already blocked by the account's data policy; PII egress guard). Not covered: output-licensing/commercial-use terms of DeepSeek and Moonshot for a paid children's product; deprecation timelines for the pinned endpoint (the V4 Pro price row is deliberately excluded from refresh_pricing auto-refresh and must be hand-repriced, pricing.py:244-251: documented but fragile); Modal endpoint economics (the S-1 Kimi leg's spend was to be "accounted from Modal billing" per the register and appears in no record; the deepseek-modal leg 503'd all day).
Recommendation: a one-page vendor-risk table per recommended stage assignment: terms, deprecation policy, swap detection, and a named cross-family fallback for review.

8. [MEDIUM] [4.2, 4.3, F7] Catalog growth cost is dominated by the human and is nowhere accounted; the arithmetic changes the "cheap tier" policy.
Demand: Q-1 exhaustion by ~4th request at 3-4 skeletons/cell; 18 offered cells implies 54-72 accepted skeletons against 20 today. Supply: tool-assisted acceptance fable/opus 6/6, sonnet 4/6, haiku 3/6, at 2-8 checker runs, with zero token accounting; S-5's amortization prices promotion review at 2 review-hours per accepted skeleton, so closing the gap costs ~68-104 review hours plus 34-104 frontier sessions. At 50 delivered books/skeleton even $10/shell of tokens amortizes to $0.20/book, so tokens are not the issue; the unpriced human hours are, and per-book approval time (F8, mandatory) is worse: UW-C02/roadmap records the review surface cannot deliver approval at 746 nodes, and no document records review minutes per book (checked PROJECT-PLAN:758, cyo-review-response-2026-08-11:202-233, S-5). At any internal rate above ~$20/hour the human approval of a 16+ book plausibly exceeds its entire LLM bill.
Recommendation: measure review minutes per book in the review UI (UW-G19 already names the field); carry review hours in the amortized model.

9. [MEDIUM] [5, 4.5] The cost-model spec already exists twice in the companions, unbuilt, uncited by the brief, and with a circular ceiling.
UW-G19 (unscheduled-work-register.md:735) specifies nearly the whole needed instrument (per-run tokens, cost, duration, retries, repairs, validator failures, human review minutes; derived cost per candidate / per validator-passing candidate / per approved book). Test plan E5 pre-registers marginal plus amortized accounting with an auditable basis. Neither is cited by 4.5 or 5. Worse, S-5's cost ceiling is relative ("request-path amortized cost <= 2x S0's amortized cost per delivered book") where S0's amortized cost has never been computed (blocked on S-1..S-4): the only pre-registered cost gate compares against an unknown.
Recommendation: cite E5/UW-G19 from 4.5; compute the S0 baseline first (inputs exist today); convert the S-5 ceiling to a dollar number.

10. [LOW] [4.5] The superseding brief's cost section is weaker than the treatment current in its own cited companions.
08-10 section 30 and the vendor README carry the stronger, still-current results: $/delivered book with failures charged to survivors ($40/1000 for V4 Pro to $1,419/1000 for Sonnet 5, a 36x spread), the reasoning-share projection rule, the cache-hit warning. A superseding "current account" whose cost section drops "price the delivered book, not the call" invites exactly the per-call misreading the companion warns against.
Recommendation: one paragraph in 4.5 restating the $/delivered-book table and its scope.

## Recommended artifact (minimal cost model)
One tracked page, docs/planning/unit-cost-model.md, plus a generated CSV; owner: core-maintainer; refresh on every pricing.py change and every live run. One row per offered cell (18); columns: commissioned words; fill input tokens (skeleton size); measured output tokens per delivered word (currently 6.0-7.9); fill $ at the pinned endpoint rate with a cache-hit term; repair rate x repair $; scrap rate charged to survivors (section-30 method); review $ (job ledger); cover $ (once metered); human review minutes x declared internal rate; skeleton amortization $ (shadow-priced authoring sessions plus 2 review-hours, over 50 books/skeleton, cross-checked against Q-1 demand); evaluation overhead amortized; total $/published book; and a cap column derived from the 20%-of-revenue target so every row shows headroom or breach. This is UW-G19 promoted to a deliverable.

Strengths
- The accounting code is disciplined and honest: None-vs-0 token semantics, unknown_calls, ANDed cost_complete, Decimal money, dated sourced prices, per-call costing, overflow capping, moderation billed to the generating job; the live run's harness figure matched OpenRouter billing within 2%.
- 4.5's headline is reproducible: $4.43/$3.52/$3.38/~90% recomputed exactly from committed records and the committed price table; the 08-10 "$/delivered book" method is strong measurement practice.
- Waste-mode forensics culture is real: AL-327/328/329/333/348 each traced a billing defect to a mechanism; run-6 self-corrected; the delivery floor calibrated against the exact failing books.

Top 3
1. Build the per-book cost model and state the cap: the 20%-of-revenue arithmetic against measured 16+ fill costs is the single most decision-relevant number never computed.
2. Record and shadow-price the subagent and Modal legs before F4/F7 harden into policy; today the regime experiment's cost side is unrecorded and irreproducible.
3. Close the production gaps the experiments already exposed: pin the OpenRouter endpoint, wire the 0.6 floor into the gate, price anthropic/modal and meter covers, add a spend rollup with an alert.
