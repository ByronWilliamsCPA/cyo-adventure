# Advisory floors replayed against production (2026-08-31)

> **Purpose**: measure what a candidate advisory floor would actually remove from a reviewer's load,
> using all 31 stored production moderation reports rather than the fixture corpus. Generated by
> `scripts/replay_production_floors.py` from
> [`production-findings-extract-2026-08-31.json`](production-findings-extract-2026-08-31.json).
> Re-run it, do not edit this file by hand.

Task `RS-CAL1` of the [review-screen remediation plan](../review-screen-remediation-plan-2026-08-31.md).
Companion to [`RS-CAL2`'s fixture-corpus derivation](stage0-floor-table-2026-08-31.md).

uv run python scripts/replay_production_floors.py \

## Headline

**The advisory floor is not the lever for reviewer load, and the ratified per-category floor removes
under 1% of it in the two bands that carry most of it.** The owner's 2026-08-31 ruling that low
advisories are "counted and available for a reviewer to dig into, but not part of the default view in
detail", already shipped as `RS-A`, removes 96% to 100%. Ordering the three candidate levers by what
they actually do to production load:

| Lever | 13-16 | 16+ | 8-11 | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RS-A` low-advisory collapse | **-97.7%** | **-96.9%** | **-99.5%** | shipped |
| `violence*` floor at 0.50 | -79.7% | -71.4% | -90.0% | not proposed |
| ratified `violence*` floor at 0.10 | -0.2% | -0.6% | -31.3% | ratified `UW-C378` |
| flat floor at 0.10 | -5.5% | -17.7% | -38.4% | rejected |

## Source data and what it can answer

31 stored reports, 5,736 nodes, 248 findings, 4,101 finding-node occurrences, all six age bands.
Two properties of the stored data bound the question:

- **The reports are truncated at the production floor.** A persisted finding is one that already
  cleared 0.01; the minimum score present is 0.0102 and nothing below it was ever written. Floors
  *below* production are unanswerable here, so the script refuses to print a row for one rather than
  emitting a figure identical to the baseline that would read as "lowering the floor changes
  nothing". Closing that gap is precisely what `RS-CAL3`'s fresh capture is for.
- **Load is occurrences and node hits, not findings.** A single merged finding spans up to 408 nodes.
  The queue's `flagged_count` counts occurrences; the reviewer walks nodes. Both are reported.

## Why the ratified floor does nothing

Every wide merged finding in the corpus has the same shape. The fourteen findings spanning 40 or more
nodes are all `source: openai`, verdict `advisory`, severity `low`, and all but one are `violence` or
`violence/graphic`. Their scores cluster at **0.33 to 0.45**:

| Band | Book nodes | Span | Category | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16+ | 505 | 408 | `violence` | 0.429 |
| 13-16 | 550 | 380 | `violence` | 0.452 |
| 13-16 | 550 | 310 | `violence` | 0.451 |
| 16+ | 314 | 288 | `violence` | 0.453 |
| 13-16 | 375 | 263 | `violence` | 0.427 |
| 16+ | 505 | 141 | `violence/graphic` | 0.351 |

A 408-node `violence` advisory at 0.429 in a 505-node book means 81% of that book's nodes score above
the floor on violence, at a typical score forty times the floor. `UW-C378` aimed its per-category
floor at exactly the right categories and set it three to four times too low to touch them. On the
fixture corpus that same split looked like a 63% noise cut, because the fixture corpus contains no
adventure book.

Whether a 16+ adventure scoring 0.43 on violence across most of its nodes is even a defect is a
policy question, not a threshold question: the classifier is arguably right, and peril is the genre.
That is the argument for the collapse rather than a floor.

## Findings

1. **The floor cannot reach the load.** In 13-16 and 16+, 0.2% and 0.6% of occurrences respectively
   sit in the `[0.01, 0.10)` window the ratified split removes. Raising the flat floor all the way to
   0.10 reaches 5.5% and 17.7%, at the recall cost `RS-CAL2` priced.
2. **A `violence*` floor near 0.50 would work**, removing 71% to 90%. It is not currently proposed
   and should not be adopted from this document alone: at 0.50 it sits inside the graded range where
   real violence findings live, so it needs the recall check `RS-CAL3`/`RS-CAL4` provide. It is
   recorded because it is the only floor in the candidate space with real leverage.
3. **The shipped collapse dominates both.** 96% to 100% across every band, with no recall cost at
   all, because a collapsed finding is still counted and still reachable. It changes what is in the
   default view, not what the gate detects.
4. **`RS-CAL3` and `RS-CAL4` should be re-scoped before spending on them.** Their value is now
   entirely about **recall** (would a different floor catch a false negative), not about reviewer
   load, which this table shows the floor cannot move and `RS-A` already moved. `RS-CAL2`
   independently found the floor is not a recall lever in the candidate range either. The paid
   capture is worth doing to price a `violence*` floor near 0.50 and to measure below 0.01, and that
   is a narrower and cheaper question than the original framing.
5. **Small bands cannot carry a per-band conclusion.** 3-5 and 5-8 hold 5 books and 157 nodes between
   them; at a 0.10 flat floor the 5-8 band drops to zero findings. Per-band floors for the young
   bands need more books, not more analysis.

## A correction to how these numbers were first derived

The first pass computed node hits in SQL as `count(DISTINCT node_id)` grouped by band. Node
identifiers repeat across books, so that silently merged different books' nodes and undercut node
hits by 8% to 14% (13-16 read 1,092 instead of 1,189). The script counts a per-book set and sums,
and `test_node_hits_are_counted_per_book_not_band_wide` pins it.
